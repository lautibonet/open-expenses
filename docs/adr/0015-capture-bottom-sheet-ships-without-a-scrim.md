# 0015 - The capture bottom sheet ships without a scrim

The capture bottom sheet declares `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"` (#103, #104) but, until #106, did not enact modal semantics: keyboard focus could Tab out of the sheet into the page behind it, and the page stayed scrollable while the sheet was open. #106 closed the keyboard and scroll gaps — the sheet traps Tab within the dialog and locks body scroll while open — and raised the question of whether the sheet should also dim the page behind it with a scrim.

We decide the sheet ships **without a scrim**. The design system builds separation from hard strokes and surface steps, not full-bleed color panels: the sheet's modal 2px On Surface top stroke against Surface Lowest is already the sanctioned boundary treatment (DESIGN.md, Hard Stroke Rule), and every existing overlay in the app (undo toast, sticky bars) sits on the page with a stroke rather than a dimming layer. A scrim would introduce the first translucent full-bleed color panel in the product and would need a new sanctioned token and interaction rule before it could be added — a cost the current sheet does not justify, since focus cannot leave the dialog by keyboard and the page cannot scroll beneath it.

Consequences: `aria-modal` is enacted purely through the focus trap and body scroll lock in `BottomSheetComponent` (#106); the page behind the sheet remains fully visible and undimmed, exactly as on desktop where the capture form is inline. If visual separation ever proves insufficient (e.g. overlapping content confusion reported on short viewports), the scrim question reopens with a new token proposal, not an ad-hoc color.
